# Completed: Fallen-Dirt-as-Terrain

**Commit:** `250bf13`

## What shipped

The reintegration step. When falling debris comes to rest against
terrain or other parts, it stops being a rigid body and becomes
terrain again — the same voxel data structure the rest of the world
lives in.

- Each falling body classifies itself per-tick into one of three
  states:
  - **Free** — moving in air; continue physics simulation.
  - **Partial-buried** — touching terrain on enough sides; freeze in
    place but stay a rigid body.
  - **Full-buried** — fully embedded; integrate as tracked SDF voxels,
    free the rigid body.

## Key decisions taken

- **Three-state classification, not just "stopped vs. moving."** The
  partial-bury freeze is a deliberate intermediate — falling material
  hitting an angled surface and resting against it shouldn't keep
  jiggling, but it shouldn't be re-absorbed into terrain unless it's
  fully embedded either.
- **Integration uses `register_voxel`** through the same machinery
  parts use. The terrain doesn't know it just absorbed a rigid body;
  it just sees new tracked cells.
- **The principle that this validates:** *matter is matter.* A dirt
  cell that came from a falling rigid body is structurally identical
  to a dirt cell that was generated by the procedural noise. The
  structural integrity system propagates support through both
  identically.

## Lessons learned

- **This is the thesis vindicated in motion.** The first time you see
  a chunk of dirt collapse, fall, partial-bury against a slope, and
  watch the rest of the dirt cone settle around it — and then dig
  through the cone and the structural integrity recomputes correctly
  — the architecture is doing exactly what the manifesto promised.
- **Partial-bury catches the long-tail physics jitter.** Without it,
  rigid bodies on sloped terrain settle into infinite tiny oscillations
  and never reach `can_sleep`. The freeze step is cheap and dramatic-
  looking ("oh, that bit got stuck there").

## Deferred

- **Falling damage** — impact crumbling dirt, splintering wood,
  chipping stone. → FEAT028.
- **Hinge-at-boundary collapse** — material with one strong attachment
  slumping rather than flying off. → FEAT029.
- **Material-mixing on integration** — falling dirt landing on stone
  should integrate as dirt-on-stone, with the material boundary
  visible. Currently it integrates as the dirt material; the boundary
  exists in data but isn't rendered distinctly.
